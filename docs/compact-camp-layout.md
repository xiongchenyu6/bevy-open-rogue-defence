# Compact camp and upgrade overlays

In short landscape windows, projected camp captions and centered upgrade notices covered combat effects and damage labels. Camp entries now use a right-side column of 40-pixel icon buttons, with full names and unlock/explore text revealed on hover or press. The existing camp choice panel and safe-reward/fight actions remain the source of challenge details. Taller viewports retain projected captions.

Skill upgrade notices move lower in short windows, into the gap before the bottom controls. Their queue, pause behavior, duration and rank/evolution descriptions are unchanged.

The native audit is queued for English and Simplified Chinese at 1280×720, 667×375 and 390×844. Two additional compact interaction captures use real virtual-window cursor movement, hover, press, the existing camp choice panel and safe reward. The initial complete library run passed 595 tests, with 0 failures and 1 existing ignored test. Its 20 native screenshots exposed poor hover contrast, so a dark padded caption background was added. After that styling-only follow-up, two camp-position tests, strict all-target Clippy and both binaries passed; the full library suite was not repeated.

Eleven final NVIDIA Vulkan cases completed 9,080 frames: six mixed-skill layouts (680 each), two compact hover/detail/safe-reward paths (1,000 each) and all three compact camp fight paths (1,000 each). Root viewed all 38 final images. Hover uses actual cursor events before the real mouse press, not direct assignment to Interaction. Every camp fight passed its existing actual arrival, phase/attack, damage, reward and cleanup assertions. Captures seed rank/offer/health prerequisites; they are not evidence of earning progression naturally.

Default compact captions no longer occupy the central combat lane; hover names/explore text are readable on the dark background, and all three icon entries open the corresponding choice panel. Upgrade notices sit below the main action and above bottom controls in the audited short viewport. Desktop and portrait comparisons retain existing behavior. Very small windows, touch hold, discoverability of locked icons and the broader compact HUD still need work; a limited layout correction is not a full mobile UI or Steam acceptance claim.
