# Infection effect readability

The previous sustained infection marker repeatedly emitted a full-height helix and radial beams through each enemy. Multiple infected targets formed a bright green mass over actor faces and bodies.

The revised marker emits short rising spores around the outside of the actor, sampled every third presentation tick (starting at tick one). Initial skill impacts retain a spreading ground pulse and a larger spore burst. Rank still scales particle size and burst count. The shared infection material uses translucent teal-green and lower emissive intensity, avoiding the opaque lime silhouette. No damage, spread generation, duration, cooldown or control formula changes.

The first full library run caught a zero-particle first presentation tick after reducing emission frequency. The sampling phase was corrected so the dense-infection regression receives feedback from its first presentation tick. This does not establish immediate per-target presentation for infections applied between sampled ticks; sustained feedback remains sampled, while actual impact requests are processed each presentation update.

The first completed native revision (r3) passed actual damage checks in both isolated infection and mixed-skill scenarios, 680 frames each on NVIDIA RTX 3070 Laptop. Root and an agent viewed all four new images against two baseline combination images. Actor silhouettes improved, but the narrow, low-opacity spores were too faint in isolation and disappeared among mixed effects. This revision was rejected for insufficient feedback, rather than counted as a final visual pass.

The next revision increases spore width/height and infection material opacity/emission, preserving peripheral placement and removing the repetitive helix. The r4 follow-up passed the dense-infection bound/pause/cleanup test, strict all-target Clippy, formatting and main/capture builds. The preceding gameplay-equivalent revision passed the complete library suite (593 passed, 0 failed, 1 ignored); that complete suite was not repeated after the material/spore-size-only adjustment. Both final NVIDIA workloads completed 680 frames and actual damage/effect checks. Root inspected all four final images and the two old combination baselines.

Final screenshots preserve enemy silhouettes and remove the opaque infection coil mass. Peripheral spores are more visible than r3 but remain understated, especially among fire/frost effects. This is a verified reduction in status clutter, not approval of the requested spectacular infection casting or overall combat art quality. Peak cast-animation visibility still needs dedicated timed captures. Dense damage-number overlap remains unresolved. Evidence is in [the verification archive](verification/virus-readability-2026-10-08/verification.json).
